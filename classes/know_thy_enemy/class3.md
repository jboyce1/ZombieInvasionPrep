# Zombie Presentations

### `1000–1030`

Each group will present its zombie.

This is a communication skill.

Your goal is not only to show what your group created. Your goal is to clearly explain your choices, your reasoning, and the connection between your zombie and the assignment.

As groups present, listen for:

* what kind of zombie they created
* what makes the zombie dangerous
* what real-world or historical ideas are present
* how the group used evidence or examples
* how clearly the group communicates its argument

# Leadership Campfire

### `1045–1105`

There is more than one way to lead.

For this discussion, we will talk about leaders who made the groups they worked with better.

We will also talk about bad leaders, damaged teams, frustration, resentment, and the importance of debriefing after difficult experiences.

As we talk, think about leaders in your own life:

* coaches
* teachers
* parents
* friends
* family members
* teammates
* club leaders
* community leaders


## Discussion Notes

Good leaders make their groups better.

Bad leaders may make the task harder, the group weaker, or the people around them less willing to contribute.

Teams need to communicate frustration before it becomes resentment.

There is a balance between speaking honestly and causing unnecessary problems.

Debriefing matters.

After difficult, stressful, embarrassing, or traumatic experiences, groups often need to talk through what happened.

Talking through what happened can help people understand the event, repair trust, learn from mistakes, and move forward.

# Group Leadership Style Reflection

### `1105–1120`

After the Leadership Campfire, each group will describe the leader or leaders who emerged during Z-Team Assignment 1.

Your group should decide which leadership style best describes your group leader.

Consider:

* Who helped the group move forward?
* Who made the group better?
* Who organized the work?
* Who challenged ideas?
* Who supported people?
* Who communicated clearly?
* Who helped the group recover from confusion or frustration?
* What leadership style best describes that person?
* Did the group need a different style of leadership at different moments?

The goal is not to flatter someone or attack someone.

The goal is to understand how leadership actually worked in your group.
