Zombie is what a Zombie does Lecture Notes

Zombie as brain puzzle

What can a zombie still do?
What does that suggest is still working?

What can a zombie not do?
What does that suggest is damaged, disconnected, or missing?

Zombie can move, turn toward sound, grab, bite, pursue people.
But movement is bad.
Stiff, clumsy, repetitive, not flexible, poor adaptation.

Movement without full thought.
Walking does not require deep conscious decision-making.
Body can run learned patterns.

Brainstem likely intact.
Basic survival functions.
Breathing, heart rate, reflexes, arousal, basic body activation.
Not “thinking,” but body still operating.

Motor system not one thing.
Motor cortex sends movement commands.
Premotor cortex prepares movement based on outside cues.
Supplementary motor area organizes repeated movement patterns.
Basal ganglia: habits, action selection, automatic routines.
Cerebellum: balance, timing, correction, coordination.
Spinal cord and brainstem: posture, reflexes, locomotion basics.

Zombie movement means some motor systems working.
Zombie movement being bad means coordination/planning/flexible adjustment damaged.

Key idea:
behavior can continue without reflection.

Zombie can walk, but not travel with human purpose.
Zombie can grab, but not understand social meaning.
Zombie can bite, but not understand moral meaning.

Prefrontal cortex likely damaged.
Decision-making, planning, impulse control, judgment, morality, social restraint.

Zombie does not stop.
Does not consider consequences.
Does not negotiate.
Does not follow social rules.
Does not inhibit appetite or aggression.

Body moves.
“Stop system” missing.

Language systems

Zombie makes sound.
Groans, growls, guttural sounds, maybe emotional tone.
Sound is not language.

Broca’s area:
speech production.
organizing language output.
turning thought into structured speech.

Zombie vocal cords may work.
Breathing/vocalization may work.
Organized speech does not.

Wernicke’s area:
language comprehension.
connecting words and sounds to meaning.

Zombie may turn toward voice.
That does not mean it understands words.
Hearing is not understanding.

Word selection:
bigger network.
Frontal lobe, temporal lobe, memory systems, language connections.
Need concept, correct word, sentence structure, meaning.

Zombie may express emotion through sound.
Not choosing words.
Not explaining, asking, persuading, remembering through speech.

Amygdala

Fear, threat detection, emotional learning, emotional memory.
What matters emotionally?
What should I react to?

Important correction:
amygdala does not store walking.
Walking/motor memory more basal ganglia, cerebellum, motor cortex, motor circuits.

But amygdala can direct attention/reaction.
Movement, sound, human voice, familiar place, familiar person, threat cues.

Zombie may not remember person normally.
Might still react to emotional importance.

Not “this is my daughter.”
More like “this matters.”

Emotional trace.
Recognition without understanding.

Conditioned emotional learning.
Cue predicts danger, reward, pain, fear, importance.
Sound, place, face, smell can carry emotional weight.

Zombie near familiar house:
not “I lived here.”
Maybe emotional tag remains.

Zombie pauses near familiar person:
not relationship memory.
Maybe emotional salience.

Motor systems make zombie move.
Amygdala may help decide what it reacts to.
Hippocampus likely damaged: no full episode memory.
Prefrontal cortex damaged: no control over reaction.

Hippocampus

Episodic memory.
Personal event memory.
Autobiographical memory.

“I lived here.”
“This is my friend.”
“This happened yesterday.”
“I remember when…”

Zombie likely lacks this.
May show familiarity, not real remembering.

Familiarity vs memory.
Something matters, but zombie does not know why.

Procedural memory

Memory for how to do things.
Habits, routines, learned actions.
Walking, reaching, grabbing, climbing, turning toward sound, following route.

Basal ganglia, cerebellum, motor cortex, movement circuits.

Body remembers how.
Person does not remember why.

Zombie may have behavior without identity.

Taboo and social control

Zombie eats people.
Strong human taboo.
Not just gross, socially destructive.

Humans survive through cooperation.
Families, groups, trust, protection, shared rules.

Taboos protect social structure.
Cannibalism, uncontrolled violence, attacking own group.
Destroys trust.

Zombie reverses human social system.
Instead of protect, consumes.
Instead of recognize group, attacks.
Instead of rules, appetite.

Prefrontal restraint gone.
Impulse not inhibited.
Emotional importance twisted into pursuit.

Zombie as social collapse.
Not just body horror.
Loss of personhood, restraint, cooperation, trust.

Big idea

Zombie still has:
brainstem functions, movement, reflexes, habits, procedural memory, emotional reactions, threat detection, limited vocalization, partial familiarity.

Zombie lacks:
judgment, moral reasoning, impulse control, planning, full language, word selection, episodic memory, self-awareness, social restraint.

What makes a person human?
Movement?
Memory?
Language?
Morality?
Self-control?
Emotional connection?
Social rules?

Zombie is scary because human body remains.
Personhood systems damaged, disconnected, missing.

A zombie is what a zombie does.
