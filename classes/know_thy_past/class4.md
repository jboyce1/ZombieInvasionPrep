# Skill: Create a Sling

### `0945–1000`

A sling supports an injured arm while professional care is arranged. During this classroom simulation:

1. Support the arm in the position found. Do not force it straight.
2. Place the open triangular bandage between the arm and chest, then bring the ends up to support the forearm.
3. Secure the sling at the side of the neck rather than directly over the spine.
4. Leave the fingers visible and check color, warmth, feeling, and comfort.
5. Loosen and adjust the sling if circulation or comfort worsens.

Do not attempt to push exposed bone back into place or straighten a suspected fracture.

# Skill: Create a Splint

### `1000–1015`

A splint limits movement and helps protect an injured area until professional care is available.

1. Control life-threatening problems first and call for help.
2. Check circulation, warmth, color, and feeling beyond the injury.
3. Pad rigid supports and place them without forcing the limb into a new position.
4. Secure the supports above and below the injury without tying directly over it.
5. Recheck circulation and feeling. Loosen the ties if either becomes worse.

All practice uses uninjured volunteers and simulated injuries under instructor direction.

Instructor references: [American Red Cross: Fractures](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/learn-first-aid/fractures){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} and [FEMA CERT Basic Training Instructor Guide](https://www.ready.gov/sites/default/files/2019.CERT_.Basic_.IG_.FINAL_.508c.pdf){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}.

# Triangulation Challenge

### `1015–1055`

Your team will use several radio reports to narrow a transmitter's possible location.

For each report:

* identify the known receiving point
* interpret the reported range or coverage information
* represent that information consistently on the map
* compare it with the other reports
* identify the overlap best supported by all available evidence

Report the proposed location only when the team can explain why the overlap is stronger than nearby alternatives. Measurement, scale, line width, and signal behavior may create an area rather than one perfect point.

# Leadership Reflection and After Action Review

### `1055–1110`

Begin with leadership:

* Who organized the evidence and kept the method consistent?
* Who checked scale, measurements, or assumptions?
* Who helped the team recover from an incorrect lead?
* When did leadership shift because the task changed?

Then complete the four-part AAR:

## What was the plan?

What was supposed to happen during the Plagues of Yore assignment and today's challenge?

## What actually happened?

What changed, and how did the group respond?

## What went well?

Which skills and teamwork practices should be repeated?

## What went wrong?

Which choices, habits, or communication problems should be avoided or changed?

Agree on one practice to repeat and one to change.

# Deliberate Skill Practice

### `1110–1120`

Choose one skill that needs improvement. Do not automatically practice the newest or most enjoyable skill.

Your group may practice an instructor-approved element from this module, such as:

* tying and checking a sling
* padding, securing, and rechecking a splint
* distinguishing incubation from latency
* interpreting communicable and infectious periods
* drawing a radio coverage area accurately
* making a concise radio report

State the specific weakness, practice it deliberately, and demonstrate one improved attempt before cleanup.
