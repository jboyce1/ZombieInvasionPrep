# Skill: Swiss Seat

### `0945–1035`

This is an instructor-led outdoor skill. A Swiss seat is an improvised rope seat used as part of a larger, properly managed rope system. Tying the seat is only one part of that system; anchors, connectors, rope condition, belay or backup systems, edge protection, commands, and supervision all matter.

## Progression

1. Watch the instructor demonstration.
2. Tie the seat at ground level using the demonstrated procedure.
3. Stop and receive an instructor inspection. Do not load an uninspected seat.
4. Clip into the instructor-managed test line and conduct a low, controlled test over the pad.
5. Observe the instructor demonstration on the approximately 45-degree grassy slope.
6. Observe the instructor demonstration at the wall.

Student rappelling is optional and is not earned by finishing quickly. A student may participate only with a signed permission slip, instructor approval, correctly fitted and inspected equipment including a helmet, the required backup system, spotters, padding, and direct supervision. Any student may stop or decline without penalty.

The instructor may end or modify the activity because of weather, equipment, footing, behavior, or any other safety concern.

# Contact and Disease Timelines

### `1035–1045`

Before the game, separate two questions:

* **When does a person show signs of disease?**
* **When can that person spread the disease to someone else?**

The **incubation period** follows exposure and ends when symptoms begin. The **infectious period** describes when the person is experiencing the disease state used in this model.

The **latency period** follows exposure and ends when the person becomes able to transmit the disease. The **communicable period** is the window in which transmission can occur.

These timelines may overlap, but they do not have to begin or end together. That difference can make an outbreak difficult to recognize and control.

# Plague Tag

### `1045–1110`

Plague Tag is a model, not a perfect simulation of a real disease.

## During the Model

* Keep your assigned disease profile private unless instructed otherwise.
* Follow the contact rules and record required contacts accurately.
* Change your behavior only when the instructor announces that enough model time has passed.
* Track symptoms and communicability separately.
* Do not assume that someone without symptoms cannot transmit disease.

After play stops, reconstruct the contact pattern and compare the different disease timelines. Look for evidence that explains why some versions spread farther, become visible sooner, or remain difficult to detect.
