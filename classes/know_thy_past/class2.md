# Skill: Check for Shock

### `0945–1000`

Shock is a life-threatening failure of adequate blood flow. A basic responder does not need to identify the exact cause before acting.

Look for a combination of:

* pale, gray, cool, or moist skin
* rapid breathing or a rapid, weak pulse
* confusion, anxiety, weakness, or reduced responsiveness
* nausea, vomiting, or unusual thirst

## Immediate Response

1. Confirm scene safety and use protective equipment when appropriate.
2. Call 9-1-1 or direct a specific person to call.
3. Control any life-threatening bleeding and provide care within your training.
4. Help the casualty rest in a safe position that supports breathing and does not worsen pain or injury.
5. Protect the casualty from becoming too cold or overheated.
6. Do not give food or drink. Reassure and monitor breathing and responsiveness until help arrives.

# Skill: Transport a Casualty

### `1000–1015`

Move a casualty only when the location is unsafe, movement is necessary to reach care, or the instructor directs the simulation. Avoid moving a possible head, neck, or spinal injury unless remaining in place creates greater danger.

For the two-person fore-and-aft carry:

* the rescuer at the head leads and supports the upper body from behind
* the second rescuer supports beneath the knees
* both rescuers use stable footing, lift with their legs, and act on one leader's commands
* the team moves with short coordinated steps and lowers together

Practice only with willing, uninjured participants under instructor supervision.

Instructor references: [American Red Cross First Aid Steps](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/first-aid/performing-first-aid/first-aid-steps){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} and [FEMA CERT Basic Training Participant Manual](https://www.ready.gov/sites/default/files/2019.CERT_.Basic_.PM_FINAL_508c.pdf){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}.

# Communicable Disease or Common Source?

### `1015–1020`

A communicable outbreak grows through transmission between people or through another transmitting organism. A common-source outbreak begins when multiple people share exposure to the same contaminated item, place, or event.

The names alone do not solve an investigation. Investigators compare:

* who became ill and who did not
* where people went
* what exposures they shared
* when symptoms appeared
* whether the pattern suggests shared exposure, transmission, or both

# Common Source Challenge

### `1020–1100`

Your team is supporting a hospital during the zombie emergency. Patient interviews contain recent travel and exposure information. Some patients display the target symptoms; others do not.

Use the provided records to identify the strongest common-source pattern. Then contact **ZULU MAIN** by radio and request action for the location your evidence supports.

Before calling, your group must be able to explain:

* which records matter
* what those records have in common
* which records are likely distractions
* why your proposed location is better supported than the alternatives

A confident answer without evidence is still only a guess.
