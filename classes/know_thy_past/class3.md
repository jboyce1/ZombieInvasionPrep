# Skill: Radio-Wave Search Areas

### `0945–0955`

A radio report can help define an area without revealing an exact position.

Reception depends on factors such as transmitter power, antenna height, distance, terrain, buildings, and interference. A station that receives a transmission is probably inside the signal's effective coverage under those conditions. A station that does not receive it may be outside that coverage or blocked by terrain or structures.

By comparing reports from several known receiving locations, a team can draw possible coverage areas and narrow the search to the overlap that best fits the evidence. This defines a **search area**, not a guaranteed point. More independent observations generally produce a more defensible area.

## Required Evidence

Use the rubric throughout the work period. Your product must address:

* a clear overview supported by citations
* incubation, symptoms, and the infectious period
* latency, transmission, and the communicable period
* severity, susceptibility, and evidence about virulence
* documented outbreaks and meaningful comparisons
* prevention or risk-reduction steps
* interesting facts with enough context to explain why they matter
* a conclusion that connects the evidence rather than repeating a list

Use teacher-approved, reliable sources. Record citations while researching instead of attempting to reconstruct them at the end.

## Work Checkpoints

* **10 minutes** — roles active, sources open, required categories assigned
* **20 minutes** — core timeline and transmission evidence located
* **30 minutes** — overview, virulence, and outbreak evidence taking shape
* **40 minutes** — prevention, implications, and visual design underway
* **50 minutes** — complete draft ready for rubric check
* **60 minutes** — final product submitted and presenters ready

# Presentations

### `1055–1135+`

Each group has approximately **4–6 minutes**. Present the most decision-relevant findings rather than reading every word from the product.

The audience should be able to identify:

* what the disease is
* how and when it spreads
* how symptoms and communicability relate
* what makes the disease more or less dangerous during a zombie emergency
* what response or prevention steps the evidence supports

Every presenter should contribute. Be prepared to identify the source for a claim when asked.
