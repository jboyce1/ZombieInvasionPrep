# Skills: Bleeding Control and Casualty Transport

### `0945–1020`

These are immediate-response skills. Your job is to recognize a serious problem, protect yourself, provide basic help within your training, and get professional responders—not to diagnose or provide advanced medical care.

All practice today uses simulated injuries. Do not apply painful pressure or perform a carry on anyone who is injured.

## 1. Determine the Severity of Bleeding

Look at the amount of blood and how it is moving:

* **Capillary bleeding** usually oozes slowly.
* **Venous bleeding** usually flows steadily.
* **Arterial bleeding** may spurt or pulse.

Treat bleeding as potentially life-threatening when it is spurting or flowing continuously, rapidly pooling or soaking materials, or accompanied by signs that the casualty is becoming pale, cool, confused, weak, or less responsive.

If bleeding may be life-threatening, send someone to call **9-1-1** and get the appropriate first-aid equipment immediately.

## 2. Apply Direct Pressure and a Pressure Bandage

1. Confirm the scene is safe. Use gloves or another barrier if available.
2. Find the source and place a clean dressing directly over it.
3. Apply firm, steady pressure. Do not repeatedly lift the dressing to check the wound.
4. When the bleeding is controlled, wrap a bandage firmly over the dressing to hold pressure in place.
5. Check beyond the bandage for normal color, warmth, and feeling. Loosen the wrap if circulation is reduced.
6. Watch for renewed bleeding and changes in responsiveness or breathing until help takes over.

For this class, tourniquets are used only during separate, instructor-led training with proper equipment.

## 3. Transport a Casualty

Do not move a casualty unless the location is unsafe, the person must be moved to reach care, or an instructor directs the movement. Avoid moving someone with a possible head, neck, or spinal injury unless remaining in place creates a greater danger.

### Two-Person Fore-and-Aft Carry

1. Explain the move and gain the casualty's cooperation when possible.
2. The rescuer at the casualty's head becomes the team leader and supports the upper body from behind, reaching under the arms and securing the casualty's forearms or wrists against the torso.
3. The second rescuer faces the casualty's feet, positions between the legs, and supports beneath the knees.
4. Both rescuers squat with stable footing and straight backs.
5. The leader gives one clear command: **“Ready—lift.”** Both rescuers lift together with their legs.
6. The leader gives the movement commands. Both rescuers use short, coordinated steps and lower the casualty together.

## Skill Check

Your team should be able to:

* state whether simulated bleeding is minor or potentially life-threatening
* begin the correct immediate response without delay
* apply and secure a pressure bandage without cutting off circulation
* assign a leader and coordinate a safe two-person lift and carry

# Group Leadership-Style Reflection

### `1045–1120`

Reflect on the leadership that actually appeared during Zombie Team Assignment #2. Discuss observable actions rather than assigning permanent labels to people.

## Identify the Leadership

* Who helped the group begin working?
* Who organized people, time, or materials?
* Who supplied ideas, evidence, or technical skill?
* Who noticed problems and helped the group adapt?
* Who supported participation or reduced conflict?
* Did leadership remain with one person or shift as the task changed?

## Identify the Style

Use the leadership-style reference to decide which style or combination of styles best describes what happened.

[Open the leadership-styles reference]({{ '/classes/know_thy_enemy/leadership_styles_drk.pdf' | relative_url }}){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}

For each style your group identifies, provide one specific example of behavior from ZTA2. A title, personality-test result, or claim that someone “was the leader” is not enough evidence.

## Compare Personality and Behavior

Discuss:

* How did group members' Big Five tendencies appear during the assignment?
* When did someone act outside the behavior the group expected from them?
* Which combination of personalities helped the group?
* Which differences created useful challenge, and which created friction?
* What did the task require from leadership that the group did not initially provide?

## Close the Reflection

Agree on and record:

* **one leadership or teamwork practice to repeat**
* **one leadership or teamwork practice to change**
* **one person who should practice a different leadership role during the next challenge**

The purpose is to make the next team performance better—not to flatter, blame, or rank group members.
