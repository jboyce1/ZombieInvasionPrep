# Skill: Taut-Line Hitch

### `0945–1010`

The taut-line hitch creates an adjustable loop that can slide when unloaded and grip the standing line when placed under tension. It is useful for tent and tarp guy lines, securing equipment, and making controlled adjustments without retying the entire knot.

## Materials

* one practice rope per student
* a fixed object or partner-held rope to act as an anchor

## Tie the Knot

1. Pass the working end around the anchor and bring it back alongside the standing part.
2. Wrap the working end around the standing part twice on the inside of the loop. Keep the two wraps neat and beside one another.
3. Cross over the first two wraps and make one final half hitch around the standing part on the outside of the loop.
4. Dress the knot so the wraps are organized and tighten it.
5. Slide the hitch to adjust the loop, then pull on the standing part to confirm that the hitch grips under tension.

## Performance Check

Before moving on, demonstrate that you can:

* tie the knot without step-by-step coaching
* identify the working end and standing part
* slide the knot when the line is unloaded
* make the knot hold when the line is tensioned
* untie the knot after loading it

# Zombie Team Assignment #2

### `1010–1110`

Your team has **60 minutes** to complete and submit Zombie Team Assignment #2.

You should already have a plan from Class 2. Retrieve that plan, confirm roles, and begin. This is execution time—not another full planning period. Change the plan only when the change helps the team meet the assignment requirements within the deadline.

## Start-Up Procedure

During the first few minutes:

1. Open the planner and rubric.
2. Restate the final product your team intends to create.
3. Confirm each person's immediate responsibility.
4. Identify the first checkpoint and begin working.

# After Action Review

### `1110–1120`

Conduct a brief internal After Action Review. The purpose is to improve the team's performance in the next challenge—not to assign blame.

Each group member should contribute to all four questions:

## 1. What was the plan?

What was supposed to happen? Briefly restate the team's intended roles, sequence of work, checkpoints, and final product.

## 2. What actually happened?

How did the work unfold? How did the plan have to change so the group could accomplish the goal within the available time?

## 3. What went well?

Which skills, decisions, communication methods, or teamwork practices helped the group? Identify practices the team should deliberately repeat in future challenges.

## 4. What went wrong?

What caused delay, confusion, duplicated effort, missing work, or reduced quality? Identify behaviors or practices the team should avoid or change during the next challenge.

## Close the Review

Agree on:

* **one practice the team will repeat**
* **one practice the team will change**

Record both decisions on the back of the planner before leaving.
