# M4 Class 5 — Present and Defend the Model

**Module**: Mathematics in Zombieland

**Focus**: final preparation, presentation, live evaluation, and lessons learned

---

## Essential Question

**Does the team's evidence justify its plan for helping humanity survive?**

## Agenda

### 0945–1120+

* **0945–1005** — Final checks and targeted Zombielympics preparation
* **1005–1100+** — Team presentations and on-the-spot rubric grading
* **After presentations** — Internal AAR and facilitated group AAR

Presentation time may extend according to the number of teams.

---

# Final Preparation

### `0945–1005`

Do not use this time to redesign the entire project. Verify that materials open, required evidence is visible, speaking responsibilities are clear, and every member can explain how the scenario, rates, graph, policy, and conclusion connect.

Students not needed for a final project repair should conduct targeted Zombielympics practice with their specialty group.

---

# MIZ Presentations

### `1005–1100+`

Each Z-Team presents its outbreak and response while the instructor scores the work using the rubric. The team must be ready to:

* establish the scenario and zombie type
* explain the state variables and justify the rates
* interpret the uncontrolled outcome
* compare the policies considered
* show how the selected response changed the model
* defend the 240-minute plan
* explain the conclusion and limitations of the model
* answer questions using evidence rather than unsupported certainty

[Open the MIZ rubric]({{ '/classes/miz/files/miz-rubric.doc' | relative_url }})

Audience members should record one decision they found convincing and one question the evidence raised.

---

# Final After Action Review

After all presentations, conduct the standard internal AAR:

1. What was the plan?
2. What actually happened?
3. What went well and should be repeated?
4. What went wrong and should be avoided or changed?

Each team leader then presents the team's most useful lesson. Close by identifying how the team's independence, conduct under stress, division of labor, and use of evidence changed across the module.
