# Humanity Under Stress

### `0945–1005`

[Watch the opening video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=b7bYc2-nUX4){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}

There will be pressure. There will be disagreement. Neither changes the obligation to treat teammates as human beings.

Discuss:

* What happens to a group when people no longer believe others genuinely have their backs?
* What is the difference between disagreeing about the work and attacking the person?
* What unresolved issues must be set aside while the team is **on**?
* How should a teammate challenge a weak decision without weakening the team?

Each Z-Team will establish three brief operating agreements: how members will disagree, how they will ask for help, and how they will respond when someone falls behind.

Problems will happen, stress will get to your group- if I do not see it happening, I will make it happen.
* group recovery is the point
* group strengthening is the point
* clear communication is the point
* following your own protocol is the point

# Mathematics in Zombieland

### `1025–1115`

Your team will create a fictional outbreak, represent it with a mathematical model, interpret what happens without intervention, and use the model to test a 240-minute response.

## Scenario Boundaries

Before leaving today, select a scenario that meets all of these conditions:

* The initial human population is between **1,000 and 10,000,000**.
* The setting is modern or reasonably near-modern: no earlier than the World War II era and no more than 30 years in the future.
* The initial zombie population is small—less than **0.01 of the human population**.
* The zombie type is explicitly defined. (Its behavior must justify the rates used in the model)

Before leaving today your group should have the following on your **initial 240 minute plan**:

* Overarching plan
* Task list
* Individual assignments

## Rubric Calibration

First, use the rubric to score the ungraded sample provided by the instructor. Cite visible evidence for each score. Then inspect the previously graded student work and compare your judgment with the instructor's marks.

Do not ask, “What did this group get?” Ask:

* What evidence earned the score?
* What was present but underdeveloped?
* Where did the story, rates, graph, and policy fail to agree?
* What would move the work up one performance level?

## Before the End of Class

* a setting, population, timeframe, and initial zombie count
* a defined zombie type
* a preliminary event at minute 0
* initial 240 minute plan
* initial project roles and Zombielympics practice roles
