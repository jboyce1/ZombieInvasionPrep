# Disaster Logistics and Recovery

**Community role:** keep people, supplies, food, facilities, and records moving safely from immediate response into recovery.

**Primary pathway:** ServSafe Food Handler Certificate

**Supporting pathway:** OSHA 10-hour General Industry course-completion card

Both pathways can be completed primarily online. They require paid, legitimate course access, so students should confirm family or school approval before beginning.

# Credential stack

## ServSafe Food Handler

The ServSafe Food Handler online course and assessment can be completed independently. The official course is approximately 60–90 minutes, covers five food-safety areas, and requires a passing assessment for a printable Certificate of Achievement. ServSafe states that there is no official prerequisite.

Study high points:

* basic food safety
* personal hygiene
* cross-contamination and allergens
* time and temperature control
* cleaning and sanitation
* safe feeding-station workflow
* documentation of temperatures, deliveries, waste, and corrective actions

[Official ServSafe Food Handler course information](https://www.servsafe.com/ServSafe-Food-Handler/Get-Certified){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}

At-home evidence:

* course dashboard or study notes
* completed food-flow diagram for a shelter meal
* temperature and sanitation log template
* printable ServSafe Food Handler Certificate of Achievement

Call the document by its correct name: a **ServSafe Food Handler Certificate**, not a Food Protection Manager certification.

## OSHA 10-hour General Industry

OSHA places no minimum age on Outreach participation, and authorized online providers can issue the 10-hour General Industry student course-completion card. OSHA emphasizes that this is voluntary hazard-awareness training, not a license or professional certification and not a substitute for an employer's site-specific training.

Study high points:

* worker rights and employer responsibilities
* hazard recognition and controls
* walking-working surfaces
* electrical safety
* personal protective equipment
* hazard communication
* materials handling and ergonomics
* emergency action plans

Use only a provider on the [official OSHA-authorized online provider list](https://www.osha.gov/training/outreach/training-providers){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}. CareerSafe is identified there as youth-focused, but students may use any currently authorized provider approved by the school or family.

At-home evidence:

* provider enrollment and progress record
* hazard assessment of a fictional shelter, warehouse, or debris-sorting site
* completed hierarchy-of-controls analysis
* official OSHA 10 student course-completion card when issued

# Practice missions

* establish a receiving, inventory, storage, issue, and return process for emergency supplies
* create a shelter feeding plan and allergen-control procedure
* lay out safe pedestrian and material flow through a sandbag operation
* build a resource-status board using NIMS terminology
* document donated goods and explain why unsolicited donations can become a logistics problem
* plan transition from immediate response to cleanup and recovery

# Zombilympics checkpoint

Complete at least one of these:

* earn the ServSafe Food Handler Certificate
* finish a documented portion of OSHA 10 and show an achievable completion schedule
* complete OSHA 10 if the course was started early enough

# May 1 cohort role

Possible supervised roles include supply check-in, inventory, runner dispatch, food-safety logging, hydration support, volunteer sign-in, PPE distribution, damage-documentation support, and demobilization checkout. Students do not enter hazardous areas or perform work prohibited for minors.

# Weekly check-in evidence

Bring one current artifact: course progress, certificate, hazard analysis, feeding plan, inventory sheet, temperature log, resource board, or recovery workflow.
