# Prepare Thyself 2

**Focus:** demonstrating IS-100 knowledge and moving from ICS to NIMS

**Skill:** recognition and response to heat injury

**Credential work:** take the IS-100 exam and begin IS-700.b

# Immediate action: IS-100 exam

Students who completed the course take the official IS-100 exam independently. The assessment verifies individual learning; students may use their course materials but may not collaborate on answers or reproduce test questions.

After the attempt:

* save the official certificate if successful
* record completion in the credential tracker
* if unsuccessful, identify course objectives to review and make a retake plan

# Skill: Heat injury

Emergency and recovery work can combine heat, humidity, protective equipment, stress, lifting, and poor sleep. Review:

* heat cramps, heat exhaustion, and heat stroke
* early behavioral changes, confusion, and loss of coordination
* hydration before work and during work
* shade, cooling, acclimatization, and work/rest cycles
* buddy monitoring and the authority to stop unsafe work
* when rapid cooling and emergency medical help are required
* why an incident safety plan must account for weather and workload

# Introduction to NIMS and IS-700

ICS organizes work at an incident. NIMS explains how the larger community makes different organizations work together.

High points to introduce:

* the whole-community approach
* shared vocabulary and interoperable communications
* resource typing, inventorying, ordering, tracking, and demobilization
* the relationship among ICS, Emergency Operations Centers, Multiagency Coordination Groups, and the Joint Information System
* mutual aid
* information management
* why volunteers report through an organization rather than self-deploying

Use a Pittsburgh flood, prolonged power outage, or missing-person search as the running example. Ask what the school, city, county, utilities, nonprofits, radio operators, public works, and families would each contribute.
