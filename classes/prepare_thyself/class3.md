# Prepare Thyself 3

**Focus:** prove the common foundation, apply ICS/NIMS, and select an individual track

**Credential work:** take the IS-700 exam

**Exercise:** incident-command tabletop

# IS-700 exam

Students who completed the course take the official IS-700 exam independently. Save the certificate or create a specific review-and-retake plan. Do not share answers or reproduce protected exam questions.

# Incident-command tabletop

Use one local scenario, such as flash flooding with a missing resident, a communications outage, or a storm that damages several blocks.

## Blackouts & Biters: quick-play option

Use **Blackouts & Biters** when students need a fast introduction before attempting a detailed tabletop. One Incident Guide builds a hidden four-card Pittsburgh scenario. Responders discuss one capability, choose which track owns it, roll a d20, and try to reveal all four problems within ten turns.

The four hidden phases are Initial Aid Crisis, Search & Drone Escalation, Signal & Coordination Failure, and Long-Haul Logistics. Capability cards identify the track that leads, what the skill can reveal, and the limits of the associated training or credential.

## Three Rivers Go Dark exercise package

* [Printable role-card deck (PDF)]({{ '/classes/prepare_thyself/files/three-rivers-go-dark/three-rivers-go-dark-role-cards.pdf' | relative_url }}){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}
* [Instructor edition and exercise-control guide (PDF)]({{ '/classes/prepare_thyself/files/three-rivers-go-dark/three-rivers-go-dark-instructor.pdf' | relative_url }}){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}

The instructor edition contains the ground truth, timed injects, model responses, safety boundaries, scoring guidance, and cut-apart controller prompts. Keep it separate from the student materials until the exercise ends.

The exercise should require students to:

* establish command and state measurable incident objectives
* identify current hazards and immediate life-safety priorities
* build an organization using only the positions needed
* assign Operations, Planning, Logistics, and Finance/Administration work
* create a communications plan
* request and track resources
* produce a public message that does not spread rumors
* plan for relief, demobilization, and recovery
* conduct a short after-action review

Change conditions during the exercise. Examples include a heat casualty, a failed radio channel, a false social-media report, a road closure, a spontaneous volunteer group, or a second operational period.

# Choose an individual track

Every student chooses one primary track. A student may list a secondary interest, but the weekly plan must concentrate on one achievable next credential or verified course component.

## [Emergency Communications]({{ '/classes/prepare_thyself/EmergencyCommunications' | relative_url }})

Best for students interested in amateur radio, SKYWARN, message handling, public information, and maintaining communications when normal systems are overloaded.

## [SAR Operations]({{ '/classes/prepare_thyself/SARoperations' | relative_url }})

Best for students interested in CalTopo incident mapping, search-base documentation, supervised drone operations, Part 107 preparation, medical readiness, and the SARTECH pathway.

## [Disaster Logistics and Recovery]({{ '/classes/prepare_thyself/DisasterLogisticsAndRecovery' | relative_url }})

Best for students interested in shelter support, emergency feeding, worker safety, resource tracking, supply chains, cleanup, and long-term recovery.

# Individual credential plan

Before leaving, record:

* primary track
* target credential or certificate
* issuing organization
* exact requirements
* age or hands-on restrictions
* expected cost and who must approve it
* first at-home action
* evidence expected by Zombilympics
* role the student could safely perform in a supervised May 1 cohort mission

# Exit evidence

* IS-700 certificate or retake plan
* tabletop role and one after-action lesson
* completed individual credential plan
