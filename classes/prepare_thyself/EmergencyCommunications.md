# Emergency Communications

**Community role:** move accurate information when ordinary systems are overloaded, damaged, or unavailable.

**Primary pathway:** amateur-radio Technician license

**Supporting pathway:** SKYWARN weather-spotter training

Most preparation for this track can be completed at home. Transmitting on amateur frequencies requires the appropriate license, and all real incident participation must be coordinated through the responsible organization.

# Credential stack

## Amateur-radio Technician license

There is no minimum age for an FCC amateur-radio license. Students can study at home, take practice examinations, and arrange an examination through a Volunteer Examiner team. An issued call sign becomes the portable credential.

Study high points:

* FCC rules and operator responsibilities
* station identification and call signs
* frequency privileges and band plans
* repeaters, simplex communication, and basic radio programming
* electrical and RF safety
* antennas, feed lines, propagation, and troubleshooting
* emergency traffic, tactical call signs, and message accuracy
* phonetic alphabet and clear voice procedure

At-home evidence:

* practice-exam history showing improvement
* a written radio go-kit inventory
* a completed message form from a simulated net
* examination registration or FCC license/call sign

Use [ARRL licensing information](https://www.arrl.org/licensing-education-training){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} and verify that an exam session is administered by an authorized Volunteer Examiner team.

## NWS SKYWARN

NWS Pittsburgh offers free training that is open to all ages and provides training certificates when needed. Students learn severe-weather recognition, weather safety, and useful reporting. Amateur radio can then become the backup path for sending those reports.

At-home evidence:

* registration or completion certificate
* spotter field-guide notes
* three correctly formatted practice reports based on provided scenarios

[NWS Pittsburgh SKYWARN training and schedule](https://www.weather.gov/pbz/skywarn){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}

# Practice missions

* run a directed radio net using classroom radios without transmitting on amateur frequencies
* relay a message through three operators and audit what changed
* create a communications plan with primary, alternate, contingency, and emergency methods
* map repeater coverage and dead zones
* staff the Communications Unit during a tabletop exercise
* combine a mock SKYWARN net with a power and cellular outage

# Zombilympics checkpoint

Complete at least one of these:

* pass the FCC Technician examination
* document three consecutive passing practice examinations and register for an exam session
* complete official SKYWARN training

# May 1 cohort role

Possible supervised roles include radio-room logging, message running, equipment accountability, weather observation, communications-plan support, and public-information monitoring. Students do not self-activate or transmit outside their privileges.

# Weekly check-in evidence

Bring one current artifact: practice score, study log, scheduled exam, call sign, SKYWARN certificate, message form, coverage map, or net-control reflection.
