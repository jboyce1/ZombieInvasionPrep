# Optional Class 4: SKYWARN Storm Lab

**Focus:** observe, report, decide, and protect

**Skill:** severe-weather recognition and actionable field reporting

**Credential opportunity:** National Weather Service Pittsburgh SKYWARN training certificate, when delivered by or coordinated with NWS Pittsburgh

# Why this belongs in the common foundation

Storm spotting combines science, communication, judgment, and public safety. NWS Pittsburgh states that its SKYWARN classes are free, open to all ages, and approximately two hours; training certificates are available when needed.

[NWS Pittsburgh SKYWARN](https://www.weather.gov/pbz/skywarn){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}

# High points

* distinguish a watch, warning, and observed hazard
* identify reportable wind, hail, flooding, and storm-damage indicators
* report what was actually observed instead of diagnosing from a dramatic-looking cloud
* include time, precise location, direction, magnitude, and source
* use safe observation positions and never chase a storm for class credit
* know when to stop observing and shelter
* understand how amateur radio operators support SKYWARN nets

# Storm report challenge

Teams rotate through simulated reports, images, maps, and warning messages. For each station they must decide:

1. Is this reportable?
2. What facts are missing?
3. How should the report be worded?
4. Where should the team shelter?
5. How would the report travel if cellular service failed?

Finish with a mock SKYWARN net. Award points for accuracy, brevity, location precision, safety decisions, and correcting a rumor—not for sounding dramatic.

# Exit evidence

* NWS certificate when the official course is offered
* one correctly formatted simulated spotter report
* one personal rule for safe observation and shelter
