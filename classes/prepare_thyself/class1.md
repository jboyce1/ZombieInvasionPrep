# Prepare Thyself 1

**Focus:** common foundation, working safely, and beginning IS-100

**Skill:** sandbagging and flood-control teamwork

**Credential work:** register for the FEMA learning system and begin IS-100.c

# Class outcomes

By the end of class, students should be able to:

* explain why the Incident Command System exists
* distinguish command from doing every task personally
* identify the five major ICS functional areas
* work safely in a sandbag-filling and placement team
* access IS-100 and leave with a realistic completion plan

# Skill: Sandbagging

Use the sandbag event to introduce operational discipline rather than treating it only as a strength contest.

High points to teach:

* conduct a quick hazard assessment before work begins
* use gloves, eye protection, safe lifting, hydration, and work/rest rotation
* establish clear roles: materials, filling, tying/folding, carrying, placement, safety, and documentation
* fill bags only enough that they can be shaped and placed securely
* build from stable ground, stagger seams, and place bags consistently
* keep routes clear and prevent exhausted students from hiding an injury to remain competitive
* count output, damaged bags, time, personnel, and safety problems

The contest score should reward safe, useful work. Suggested categories are correct fill, stable placement, teamwork, documentation, and production rate. A safety violation should outweigh a small speed advantage.

# Common core: Why ICS?

Introduce IS-100 through a simple question: **How do many willing people help without making an emergency more chaotic?**

Discuss:

* common terminology
* chain of command and unity of command
* manageable span of control
* incident objectives
* accountability
* modular organization
* transfer of command
* the Incident Commander, Command Staff, and General Staff

Connect each concept back to the sandbag operation. Who set the objective? Who watched safety? Who tracked resources? When did the team become too large for one leader?

# FEMA account and IS-100 launch

Students will need a FEMA Student Identification number to take the official exam. Account creation should follow school technology rules and family-consent requirements. Do not collect student passwords, security answers, or identity documents.

1. Open the [official IS-100.c page](https://training.fema.gov/programs/independent-study/courseoverview.aspx?code=IS-100.c&lang=en){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}.
2. Confirm access to the course and identify how the final exam will be reached.
3. Begin the interactive course.
4. Record a personal plan for finishing the approximately two-hour course before Class 2.

# Exit evidence

Submit or show:

* an IS-100 progress screen or course notes
* the scheduled time and place for finishing the course
* one sentence connecting an ICS principle to the sandbag operation

Do not submit a FEMA password or other private account information.
