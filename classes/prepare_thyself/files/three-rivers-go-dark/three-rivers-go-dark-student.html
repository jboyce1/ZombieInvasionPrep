A Pittsburgh Zombie Response & Recovery Tabletop Exercise

STUDENT EXERCISE BOOK
DO NOT OPEN IN THE TUNNELS

Operational Period: 30 April–1 May, 20XX

Your mission: turn scattered skills and credentials into one functioning emergency organization.

Exercise rules

This is fiction. You are solving a classroom emergency. Do not self-deploy, contact 911, transmit on restricted radio frequencies, fly a drone, or enter a dangerous location because of this exercise.

How the tabletop works

  1. Read only the information your instructor releases.
  2. Stay in your assigned role. Give advice within your capability.
  3. Route decisions through the incident organization.
  4. Write down important messages, resources, locations, and assumptions.
  5. Expect incomplete and contradictory information.
  6. When a new inject arrives, update the plan—do not pretend the old plan still works.

Success conditions

  • Protect life without creating new victims.
  • Maintain accountability for teams and resources.
  • Build a common operating picture.
  • Communicate verified information.
  • Support survivors through stabilization and early recovery.
  • Explain why your team accepted some risks and rejected others.
Remember: Pittsburgh is dark. The rivers carry wreckage. Something keeps striking the doors below street level. The dead are frightening; confusion is more dangerous.

Exercise convention: a bite or blood exposure is a medical emergency, not permission to harm or abandon a person.

Situation: 30 April, 07:00

Three nights ago, emergency departments reported patients with extreme fever, loss of speech, and violent convulsions. By midnight, 911 calls overwhelmed the system. Before sunrise, electrical failures rolled through the city.

The Fort Pitt and Liberty tunnels are closed. Abandoned vehicles jam the Parkway East. A dark column of smoke rises beyond the Strip District. The Monongahela carries furniture, trash, and one empty ambulance with its rear doors open. From somewhere under downtown comes the metallic groan of a train that should not be moving.

Your school has become Three Rivers Coordination Site, a supervised neighborhood support point outside the immediate hot zone. Adult public-safety agencies remain in authority, but communications are intermittent. Your student cohort is assigned safe support functions: information, mapping, logistics, documentation, weather observation, shelter support, and supervised technical tasks.

Verified at 07:00

  • 62 survivors are at the site; 11 are children under ten.
  • Two adults have non-life-threatening injuries. One is feverish and has blood on a sleeve; the source is unknown.
  • Commercial power is out. Generator fuel is estimated at six hours.
  • Cellular service is unreliable. A local amateur-radio repeater is operating.
  • Heavy rain is forecast. Storm drains are clogged with debris.
  • A message reports six residents stranded on the upper floor of a senior building near the Monongahela.
  • No student team may enter a hot zone or leave without instructor authorization.

Exercise objectives

  1. Establish an ICS organization and three incident objectives.
  2. Verify and map survivor, hazard, and resource information.
  3. Create a communications and public-information plan.
  4. Support a supervised search or reconnaissance plan without exceeding credentials.
  5. Operate shelter, feeding, supply, and worker-safety systems.
  6. Plan the transition from response to recovery by May 1.

Cut-apart role cards

The certifications printed on a card are your credentials inside this exercise. They do not grant real-world authority.

Incident Commander

COMMON FOUNDATION

Scenario credentials: FEMA IS-100 and IS-700.

Capabilities: establish objectives, approve the organization and assignments, set priorities, request adult-agency support, and authorize information release with PIO advice.

Your first questions:

  • What threatens life now?
  • What do we know versus assume?
  • Who is responsible for each objective?

Limits: You do not do every task or override safety rules. You cannot authorize students into a hot zone.

Secret pressure: Everyone keeps asking you for instant answers. Demand short recommendations and make a decision.

Safety Officer

COMMON FOUNDATION

Scenario credentials: FEMA IS-100/700; heat-injury and sandbag safety training.

Capabilities: identify hazards, recommend controls, create work/rest rules, stop unsafe student activity, and investigate near misses.

Watch for: blood exposure, heat/cold stress, fatigue, floodwater, smoke, traffic, unstable structures, noise attraction, and missing accountability.

Limits: You advise on risk; medical diagnosis and law-enforcement decisions belong to qualified adults.

Power: Say “STOP WORK” when an assignment exceeds the group’s capability.

Public Information Officer

COMMON FOUNDATION / COMMUNICATIONS

Scenario credentials: FEMA IS-100/700; message-verification practice.

Capabilities: prepare verified public updates, correct rumors, coordinate one public voice, and separate confirmed facts from reports.

Every release includes: what happened, what people should do, where help is available, time of update, and next update time.

Limits: Do not publish victim names, student locations, tactical routes, or unverified infection claims.

Challenge: A terrifying story can spread faster than a zombie. Accuracy is a life-safety tool.

Liaison Officer

COMMON FOUNDATION

Scenario credentials: FEMA IS-100/700.

Capabilities: maintain partner contacts, identify what each organization can provide, record commitments, and route requests through command.

Partners: city/county emergency management, fire/EMS, hospitals, public works, utilities, NWS, amateur radio, food banks, schools, and voluntary organizations.

Limits: You cannot promise resources another organization has not approved.

First product: a partner list containing capability, contact method, request, and status.

Communications Unit Leader

EMERGENCY COMMUNICATIONS

Scenario credentials: FCC Technician license; FEMA IS-100/700.

Capabilities: design primary/alternate/contingency/emergency communications, assign channels, manage radio equipment, and maintain a communications log.

License meaning: the Technician license permits operation only within FCC amateur privileges. Emergency need does not erase all rules.

Limits: protect sensitive information and do not interfere with public-safety frequencies.

First product: a communications plan for command, shelter, field teams, and partners.

Net Control / Radio Operator

EMERGENCY COMMUNICATIONS

Scenario credentials: FCC Technician license; radio net and message-form practice.

Capabilities: open and close a directed net, prioritize traffic, relay messages exactly, track stations, and request repeats.

Use: call sign, destination, precedence, time, concise message, confirmation.

Limits: You do not invent missing facts or transmit outside license privileges.

First task: start a station log and require every field message to have a time and source.

SKYWARN Spotter

EMERGENCY COMMUNICATIONS

Scenario credentials: NWS Pittsburgh SKYWARN training certificate.

Capabilities: observe from safety, distinguish reportable hazards, and submit time/location/magnitude/source reports through approved channels.

Certificate meaning: you are trained to make useful reports; it does not make you a meteorologist or authorize storm chasing.

Limits: Never leave shelter to improve a report. Do not estimate what you cannot observe.

Watch: rainfall, rising water, wind damage, hail, lightning, and warning updates.

Message Logger / Runner

EMERGENCY COMMUNICATIONS

Scenario credentials: ICS message-handling practice; no radio license required for non-transmitting duties.

Capabilities: time-stamp messages, preserve exact wording, obtain signatures, deliver paper traffic, and close the loop when action is completed.

Limits: Do not change priority, add rumors, or take a shortcut through a restricted area.

Your rule: If it was not logged, command may not know it happened.

First task: number every message and build an incoming/outgoing log.

Planning / CalTopo Map Unit

SAR OPERATIONS

Scenario credentials: CalTopo for Incident Response / NASAR completion certificate; FEMA IS-100/700.

Capabilities: maintain the common operating picture, create operational periods, mark clues and hazards, build search assignments, and produce map packets.

Certificate meaning: you completed incident-mapping training; it does not make you a field searcher or incident commander.

Limits: protect private locations and label unverified reports clearly.

First product: a time-stamped map of sectors, hazards, teams, survivors, and unknowns.

Search-Base Documentation

SAR OPERATIONS

Scenario credentials: SARTECH awareness/preparation; ICS documentation practice.

Capabilities: issue assignment numbers, record team members and equipment, support briefing/debriefing, log clues, and track departure/return.

Training meaning: you understand search-base workflow; it does not authorize unsupervised field search.

Limits: A team with no assignment, supervisor, communications, or return time does not deploy.

Power: Ask, “Who has them, where are they, and when do we expect them back?”

Drone Mission Planner / Visual Observer

SAR OPERATIONS

Scenario credentials: Part 107 study completion; supervised flight training. Adult instructor is remote PIC.

Capabilities: analyze airspace/weather, plan a safe search grid, inspect equipment, act as visual observer, and log images and findings.

Training meaning: you may support and, under direct PIC supervision, manipulate controls. You are not the remote PIC.

Limits: PIC approves every flight. Maintain visual line of sight; avoid people, emergency aircraft, smoke, and unsafe weather.

First product: mission objective, area, hazards, altitude, abort criteria, and image-log plan.

Medical Support Lead

SAR OPERATIONS

Scenario credentials: recognized CPR/AED and basic first-aid course-completion card.

Capabilities: perform within training, organize a treatment area, request EMS, use PPE, track patients, and advise on first-aid supplies.

Card meaning: you demonstrated basic lifesaving skills; it does not permit diagnosis, independent medical practice, or entering an unsafe scene.

Limits: Scene safety first. Use universal precautions. Fever and exposure decisions require adult medical/public-health direction.

First task: separate immediate first aid, routine needs, and possible exposure while preserving dignity.

Operations / Field Team Coordinator

SAR OPERATIONS

Scenario credentials: FEMA IS-100/700; SARTECH preparation; land-navigation and radio practice.

Capabilities: turn objectives into supervised assignments, check team readiness, coordinate safe reconnaissance, and report status to command.

Limits: No student enters a hot zone, unstable building, floodwater, smoke, or zombie-contact area. Training is not operational authority.

First questions: What is the objective? Who supervises? What is the communications plan? What triggers withdrawal?

Power: Recommend a remote method when field risk is unacceptable.

Clue & Evidence Recorder

SAR OPERATIONS

Scenario credentials: CalTopo clue-object and SAR documentation training.

Capabilities: assign clue numbers, record exact location/time/source, photograph only when authorized, preserve chain of information, and update Planning.

Limits: Do not touch blood, bodies, weapons, medicine, or unknown substances. Do not publish disturbing images.

First task: create a clue log that distinguishes observation, inference, and rumor.

Warning: The wet handprint on the inside of a locked door is a clue—not an invitation.

Logistics Section Lead

DISASTER LOGISTICS & RECOVERY

Scenario credentials: FEMA IS-100/700; resource-management training.

Capabilities: organize communications, food, supplies, facilities, transport, personnel support, and demobilization.

Limits: Logistics finds and tracks support; it does not send people on unapproved missions or accept unsafe donations.

First product: resource inventory with quantity, location, condition, owner, request status, and return plan.

Problem: Everyone wants the last batteries. Make allocation follow incident priorities.

Supply & Inventory Manager

DISASTER LOGISTICS & RECOVERY

Scenario credentials: NIMS resource-management practice; OSHA hazard-awareness training.

Capabilities: receive, inspect, label, store, issue, recover, and document supplies; establish reorder points.

Limits: Quarantine leaking, spoiled, unlabeled, blood-contaminated, or suspicious donations. Do not lift beyond safe limits.

First task: count water, food, PPE, batteries, medical supplies, sanitation supplies, fuel, radios, and sandbags.

Your enemy: a mountain of donated junk blocking the fire exit.

Shelter & Feeding Lead

DISASTER LOGISTICS & RECOVERY

Scenario credentials: ServSafe Food Handler Certificate; shelter-support orientation.

Capabilities: design safe food flow, prevent cross-contamination, monitor time/temperature, manage allergens, and keep sanitation logs.

Certificate meaning: you demonstrated food-handler knowledge; you are not a Food Protection Manager or medical professional.

Limits: Reject unsafe food. Keep possible infection isolation separate from food preparation and general shelter.

First product: feeding schedule, allergy check, handwashing plan, temperature log, and waste route.

Worker Safety & Recovery Lead

DISASTER LOGISTICS & RECOVERY

Scenario credentials: OSHA 10 General Industry course-completion card; sandbag and heat-injury training.

Capabilities: recognize hazards, recommend controls and PPE, design safer work flow, document near misses, and support cleanup planning.

Card meaning: OSHA 10 is hazard-awareness training—not a license, certification, or permission for prohibited work.

Limits: Minors do not perform hazardous demolition, electrical repair, body recovery, confined-space entry, or biohazard cleanup.

First product: hazard-control plan using elimination, substitution, engineering, administrative controls, and PPE.
