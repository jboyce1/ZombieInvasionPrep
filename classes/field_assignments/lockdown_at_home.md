# Asynchronous Mission 1: Lockdown at Home

**Your question:** If everyone in your home had to stay inside—with no electricity and no running water—how long could your household remain there **safely**?

**Your products:**

1. Complete the [editable investigation workbook (Word)]({{ '/classes/field_assignments/files/lockdown-at-home-student-workbook.docx' | relative_url }}). Type in it with Word or LibreOffice, or print it and write by hand.
2. Turn your most important findings into the [editable presentation template (PowerPoint)]({{ '/classes/field_assignments/files/lockdown-at-home-presentation-template.pptx' | relative_url }}).

The **workbook** is your detailed evidence for the teacher. The **presentation** is your clear, privacy-safe household briefing. Do not try to squeeze the entire inventory into the slides.

**Time:** about 2–3 hours. You may divide the work across several days.

<div class="warning-box">
<strong>The zombies are the MacGuffin.</strong> They make the scenario interesting; your plan should work during a disease outbreak, water-main failure, blackout, winter storm, flood, or other real emergency. This is planning—not a prediction that society will collapse.
</div>

## Situation report

At **6:00 p.m. tonight**, officials issue a shelter-in-place order. Everyone who normally lives in your home is inside. No one may leave. Electricity, tap water, sewer service, internet, and cell service stop. You may use only what is already in the home. The event has no known end date.

Assume the building itself is safe. Do not buy anything for this assignment. Do not test unsafe equipment, eat emergency food, open sealed supplies, handle weapons, or interrupt medication use.

## Before you begin: protect your household

Ask an adult for permission before looking through shared spaces. Estimate when an exact count would invade someone’s privacy.

**Never put these in your schoolwork:** your street address, alarm or lock details, hiding places, valuables, weapons, medication names, medical diagnoses, family passwords, or a map showing entry points. Write general labels such as “daily prescription—adult manages,” “locked adult-only item,” or “two secure exterior doors.”

If an adult does not want you to inspect something, record **“not inventoried—adult decision”** and continue. You will not lose credit.

# Phase 1 — Establish the household

In **Workbook Section 1**, record:

* number of adults, teenagers, younger children, and pets—no names;
* needs that change the plan, such as mobility, refrigeration-dependent supplies, infant care, allergies, dietary needs, or pet care—no diagnoses;
* one reasonable assumption you must make;
* the time your scenario begins.

Then write a one-sentence decision statement:

> We need to decide how long our household of ___ people and ___ pets can safely shelter without utilities and what agreements would make that time safer.

# Phase 2 — Build an honest inventory

Use **Workbook Sections 2–4**. Count what is available now. Do not count an item twice and do not assume you can buy, collect, or borrow it later.

## Water

Use **one gallon per person per day** as your planning baseline. This includes drinking and sanitation. More may be needed for illness, heat, pregnancy, pets, or other needs. Read [Ready.gov: Water](https://www.ready.gov/water){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} and [CDC: Creating and Storing an Emergency Water Supply](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/how-to-create-and-store-an-emergency-water-supply.html){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}.

Count sealed drinking water first. You may list other water separately, but do not call it drinkable unless an official source supports the treatment method. Never drink pool or flood water. Never mix bleach with another cleaner.

> **Baseline water-days = safe gallons available ÷ number of people**

List pets separately and identify the source you used for their needs.

## Food

Inventory food that can be eaten safely with **no electric stove, microwave, refrigerator, or freezer**. Group it instead of listing every brand: ready-to-eat meals, canned food, grains/crackers, nut or seed products, fruit, infant food, pet food, and so on.

Check whether you have a manual can opener. Account for allergies and dietary requirements. Use the [USDA emergency food-safety guide](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/emergencies/food-safety-emergency){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} to decide what refrigerated and frozen food can safely count.

Choose a transparent counting method—meals, servings, or calories—and show the arithmetic. Do not use extreme calorie restriction. This is an academic estimate, not permission to change anyone’s diet.

> **Food-days = usable household food units ÷ household units needed each day**

## Health, light, information, and basic needs

Count categories, not private details:

* first-aid and hygiene supplies;
* toilet paper, soap, menstrual products, diapers, and pet-waste supplies where relevant;
* flashlights, batteries, safe backup lighting, blankets, and season-appropriate clothing;
* battery or hand-crank radio, walkie-talkies, charged power banks, and paper contact information;
* needed supplies that depend on refrigeration, electricity, or adult management;
* morale items that help people manage stress without power.

Use the [Ready.gov emergency supply list](https://www.ready.gov/kit){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} as a comparison. A missing item is useful evidence—not a reason to hide the gap.

# Phase 3 — Solve the sanitation problem

In **Workbook Section 5**, explain what your household will do if toilets, sinks, trash pickup, and sewer service do not work.

Your plan must address:

1. hand cleaning;
2. a designated toilet arrangement;
3. separation of human waste from food, water, children, pets, and living areas;
4. containment of trash and odors;
5. the point at which sanitation makes the home unsafe.

Start with [CDC: Water Emergency Safety Guidelines](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/safety/index.html){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} and [CDC: Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene Emergencies](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}. Follow local public-health instructions during a real event. Do not bury, burn, flush, or dump waste as part of this assignment. Do not mix cleaning chemicals.

# Phase 4 — Make decisions before pressure rises

Use **Workbook Sections 6–8**. For every decision, state the rule, the reason, and a trigger that would cause the household to reconsider.

## Rationing rule

Choose and defend one approach:

* begin a moderate plan on Day 1;
* eat normally until a pre-decided trigger;
* use stages that change at set supply levels.

Water needed for health and sanitation is not a bargaining prize. Children, medical needs, exertion, heat, and pets may make equal portions unfair. Identify who monitors supplies and how disagreements are handled.

## People and roles

Assign responsibilities by ability, not stereotype. Include backups. Possible functions are coordination, water/food log, sanitation, health check, information/communications, pet or dependent care, and safety inspection.

No minor should be assigned to confront an intruder, operate a fuel-burning device, manage another person’s medication, or perform hazardous waste work.

## Neighbors and mutual aid

Choose **one to three nearby households by relationship only** (“upstairs neighbor,” not names or addresses). For each, weigh likely benefits, costs, boundaries, and what would earn or reduce trust.

Write a pre-event agreement covering:

* what information or resources could be shared;
* what remains private;
* how requests are made and recorded;
* whether help is a gift, exchange, or shared pool;
* what no household is required to surrender;
* how either side may end the arrangement safely.

Then answer: **Is this more likely to become “every household for itself,” mutual aid, or something in between? What evidence or assumptions support your view?**

## Safety and security

Build a lawful, nonviolent plan: keep doors and windows secured, maintain privacy about supplies, use a buddy system, check on neighbors without entering danger, establish a family code word, and create a retreat/shelter location inside the home. Plan how to contact emergency services when communication returns.

Do **not** propose traps, patrol confrontations, vigilante action, or instructions for using weapons. If weapons exist, describe only an adult-controlled storage boundary—never location or operation. Review [Ready.gov: Make a Plan](https://www.ready.gov/plan){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}.

# Phase 5 — Plan communications

In **Workbook Section 9**, create a PACE plan: **Primary, Alternate, Contingency, Emergency**.

Consider cell/text, an out-of-area contact, battery radio, NOAA Weather Radio, walkie-talkies, a written message location, and an in-person signal that does not reveal private information. Radios do not all reach the same distance, and some radio services require licenses.

Use [Ready.gov: Family Emergency Communication Plan](https://www.ready.gov/plan-form){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} and [National Weather Service: NOAA Weather Radio](https://www.weather.gov/nwr/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}. Do not transmit on a radio for this assignment.

# Phase 6 — Find the bottleneck

In **Workbook Section 10**, show your estimates for water-days, food-days, sanitation-days, critical-supply days, and safe-temperature days.

> **Safe shelter time = the lowest defensible number of days**

Explain why that lowest number is your bottleneck. Then identify:

* one no-cost change that could extend the time;
* one low-cost future purchase, with an estimated price;
* one agreement or relationship that matters more than an object;
* the trigger that would mean sheltering at home is no longer safe—without pretending evacuation is possible in this scenario.

# Phase 7 — Build and give the household briefing

Use **Workbook Section 11** to plan your presentation. The PowerPoint provides a suggested 12-slide structure, but its content boxes are guides—not forms. You may delete, resize, combine, or redesign them. You may also use a different presentation program or create your own deck.

Your presentation should communicate:

* who the plan serves, without private details;
* your safe-shelter estimate and bottleneck;
* the water and food math supporting the estimate;
* sanitation and other basic needs;
* rationing, roles, fairness, and decision triggers;
* your claim about neighbors and mutual aid;
* the PACE communication plan and nonviolent safety boundaries;
* prioritized improvements;
* a usable Day 1 household plan;
* three decisions worth making now;
* the sources that affected your conclusions.

The presentation is a **briefing**, not a copy of the workbook. Favor large text, visible calculations, short claims, and a few useful visuals. Credit any image you use.

Invite feedback; do not demand that your household adopt the plan. Record one response in the workbook: what the person agreed with, questioned, or changed. If no household member is available, review the pitch yourself and identify its weakest assumption.

# What to submit

Submit:

* your completed investigation workbook, either as a Word file or clearly photographed/scanned handwritten pages; and
* your completed presentation, either using the supplied PowerPoint or another slide format your teacher can open.

Together, your files must include visible arithmetic, at least **three linked sources**, a defensible bottleneck, pre-decided rules, and a clear household briefing.

Before submitting, check:

* [ ] I did not include names, an address, diagnoses, medication names, security details, valuables, or weapon information.
* [ ] I separated facts, estimates, and assumptions.
* [ ] I showed how I calculated water-days and food-days.
* [ ] I planned for sanitation, trash, communication, roles, neighbors, rationing, and lawful nonviolent security.
* [ ] My safe-shelter estimate uses the **weakest** system, not my most optimistic number.
* [ ] My presentation is respectful, realistic, legible, and understandable without reading the workbook.

## If software, printing, or internet access is limited

The Word workbook can be edited locally in Microsoft Word or free software such as LibreOffice. It may also be printed if printing is available, but printing is not required. If you cannot edit the file, follow its section headings on your own paper and submit clear photographs or scans.

The instructions on this page contain the complete assignment. If outside sources are temporarily unavailable, use the planning baselines printed here and mark outside information as **“source unavailable.”** Tell your teacher what format you were able to use; the format should not prevent you from demonstrating the learning.
