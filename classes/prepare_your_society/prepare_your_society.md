# M5 — Prepare Your Society

## The Constitutional Convention

The zombie invasion is over, but the former country is gone. Communities survived under different rules, old borders no longer describe functioning society, and no person or institution has an unquestioned right to govern.

Your class has four meetings to decide whether these communities can become one society again.

This is a whole-class assignment. You will not receive a daily recipe, assigned committees, designated leaders, or a model constitution. You have already practiced leadership, planning, research, specialized skills, formal procedure, and after-action review. Now you must decide how to use them.

## Your mission

Establish a legitimate process for rebuilding a shared society. Research how real societies responded after mass violence, institutional collapse, dictatorship, genocide, or civil conflict. Use that evidence to propose a path toward cohesion and reconciliation. Draft and decide whether to ratify a governing agreement capable of earning public trust without repeating the abuses of the collapse.

## The four outcomes

By the end of Class IV, the convention must present:

1. **A public account of the collapse** — what happened, what failed, and what harms the new society must face.
2. **A research-informed recovery record** — evidence from multiple historical cases and your judgment about what succeeded, failed, or remains contested.
3. **A plan for cohesion and reconciliation** — how the society will address truth, accountability, victims, displaced people, former combatants, property, trust, and prevention.
4. **A governing agreement** — a constitution or comparable foundational document that defines legitimate authority, limits power, protects people, and provides a peaceful future.

You must also maintain evidence of your individual skill practice and complete a final Big Five leadership reflection.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

* Treat historical victims and living communities with dignity.
* Do not use classmates as fictional victims, perpetrators, or targets.
* Do not create graphic descriptions of violence.
* Historical claims require credible sources.
* Real medical care, radio transmission, drone operation, field searching, and hazardous work remain within the safety and credential limits taught throughout the course.
* The deadline is the end of Class IV. The teacher will clarify facts and protect safety, but will not organize the convention for you.

## Assessment

You are being assessed on historical understanding, the quality and legitimacy of the agreement, the response to the harms of the collapse, collective leadership and collaboration, and reflection and self-understanding.

# Situation Report: The Long Silence

The first wave arrived between elections. Within months, federal and state communication failed. Some officials died at their posts. Others fled, issued contradictory orders, or used emergency authority to protect only their supporters. No lawful national election has been held for eight years. No surviving official can demonstrate an unbroken, publicly accepted right to rule.

People survived in isolated settlements. Some communities shared food, protected strangers, and preserved records. Others closed their borders. Armed groups seized warehouses, homes, vehicles, medicine, and farms. People were killed, detained without hearings, driven from their neighborhoods, abandoned during evacuations, or denied supplies because they belonged to the wrong family or settlement. Children were separated from caregivers. Property and identity records were destroyed. Rumors became accusations; accusations sometimes became punishment. Some people committed serious abuses for power or revenge. Others broke old laws to keep people alive.

The old boundaries no longer make sense. Rivers changed course, roads disappeared, and entire towns emptied. Several new settlements contain people from many former states. Courts, police departments, schools, utilities, and public-health systems operate unevenly or not at all. Former fighters still possess weapons. Displaced families want to return home. Current occupants insist that they saved and rebuilt abandoned property. Survivors want truth and accountability, but they disagree about punishment, amnesty, restitution, and forgiveness.

The immediate zombie threat has receded enough for travel and communication to resume. Delegates from the surviving settlements agree that permanent emergency rule, revenge, and fragmentation cannot continue. They have asked your convention to create the basis of a shared society. They have not agreed on who belongs, who votes, where borders lie, how leaders are chosen, how much authority government should possess, or how future abuse will be prevented.

There is no government above you to approve your work. Legitimacy must come from the process you create and the agreement people are willing to accept.

## Daily independent fieldwork

Every session begins with protected time for individual development. Choose work that advances your own track, responsibly explores another track, or examines how a course skill appeared in credible news from the previous seven days.

Keep a brief field log containing:

* date and activity;
* skill or track;
* evidence of what you practiced, explored, or found;
* what the skill accomplished or failed to accomplish;
* one next step or question.

Your fieldwork belongs to you. It does not need to match the convention’s work that day.
