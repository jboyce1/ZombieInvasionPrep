Historical UnderstandingThe convention uses history to make perceptive judgments about rebuilding society.Historical evidence meaningfully informs the convention’s choices.Research is present but does not consistently shape decisions.Historical references are incomplete, weakly connected, or unreliable.The response shows little meaningful engagement with historical recovery.
Legitimacy and the Governing AgreementThe agreement offers a compelling and coherent basis for legitimate shared government.The agreement presents a workable basis for authority, limits, and public trust.The agreement addresses the central problem but contains important gaps or tensions.The agreement is partial, contradictory, or difficult to use.The convention does not produce a coherent response to the need for government.
Recovery, Rights, and ReconciliationThe response confronts past harm while creating credible conditions for dignity, justice, cohesion, and non-recurrence.The response addresses major harms and proposes a thoughtful path toward recovery.The response recognizes harms but treats recovery unevenly or incompletely.The response avoids difficult harms or relies on vague promises.The response does not meaningfully address the consequences of the collapse.
Collective LeadershipThe class becomes a purposeful political body capable of organizing itself, using disagreement, adapting, and completing shared work.The class establishes a functional process and works productively toward its mission.The process sometimes supports the mission but depends on uneven participation or direction.The group struggles to organize, adapt, or sustain shared responsibility.The group does not establish a sufficiently functional collective process.
Individual Leadership and ReflectionThe student contributes with unusual self-awareness, responsibility, judgment, and responsiveness to the group’s needs.The student contributes reliably and explains their behavior with specific evidence.The student participates and reflects, though evidence or follow-through is uneven.The student’s contribution or reflection is limited and frequently dependent on prompting.The student provides little evidence of contribution, responsibility, or reflection.
