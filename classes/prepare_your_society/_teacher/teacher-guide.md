# Prepare Your Society — Instructor Control Guide

## Purpose

This capstone tests whether students can create the structure that earlier modules supplied. Product quality matters, but the central evidence is how the whole class functions when the goal is clear and the route is not.

## Daily rhythm

Protect 15–20 minutes for individual skill practice, cross-track exploration, or analysis of a credible news item from the preceding seven days. Class IV may use 10–15 minutes. Students maintain their own short field logs. The remaining time belongs to the convention, with approximately five minutes to preserve work and reflect.

The current-events option is analysis rather than headline collection. Look for a dated source, the relevant course skill, what that skill accomplished or failed to accomplish, uncertainty or limits in the report, and a sensible next question.

## Teacher stance

Clarify established facts, safety requirements, source quality, vocabulary, and the final deadline. Do not assign officers, committees, roles, speaking order, voting thresholds, research cases, constitutional articles, or intermediate deadlines.

When asked for organizational direction, return responsibility with questions such as:

* What work has the convention identified?
* What decision have you already made about that?
* Who has authority to answer under your process?
* How will the group preserve the decision?
* What consequence follows if this remains unresolved?

Allow inefficiency, imperfect leadership, failed motions, forgotten tasks, and repairable conflict to produce consequences. Intervene for physical or emotional safety, demeaning conduct, graphic or identity-targeted simulation, dangerous skill practice, persistent exclusion, or serious historical misinformation.

## Historical and ethical boundaries

Students may analyze violence, displacement, unlawful detention, theft, abandonment, discrimination, institutional abuse, and failures of justice without inventing graphic scenes. Never assign students identities as victims or perpetrators. Reconciliation must not be treated as required forgiveness. Amnesty, punishment, truth-seeking, restitution, reparation, institutional reform, and reintegration should remain distinguishable choices.

## Assessment approach

Use the private observation rubric as an evidence inventory, not a scavenger-hunt score sheet. Not every behavior must occur. Record the context, student action, effect on the group, and whether the student adapted. Compare the collective product with the legitimacy of the process that produced it.

Suggested evidence balance:

* 35% collective process and leadership
* 25% collective public products
* 20% individual contribution and fieldwork
* 20% Big Five leadership reflection

A failed ratification can meet or exceed expectations when it results from principled analysis and an honest process. Ratification should not conceal shallow research, concentrated control, coerced agreement, or unresolved contradictions.
