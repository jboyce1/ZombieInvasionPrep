Zombie Invasion Prep addresses Pennsylvania Social Studies standards through project-based study of government, citizenship, history, geography, research, and psychology. Students use a fictional zombie crisis to analyze why societies need rules and leadership, how communities respond to disaster, how fear and anxiety affect human behavior, how historical and cultural ideas about zombies develop, and how evidence can be used to support complex arguments.

---

# Primary Standards Areas

* Civics and Government
* History
* Geography
* Reading and Writing in History/Social Studies
* Psychology and Social Science

---

# Civics and Government

## 5.1.9.A — Necessity of Government

Students examine why groups need rules, leadership, authority, cooperation, and decision-making systems during crisis.

## 5.1.9.B — Rule of Law

Students analyze what happens when law, norms, and enforcement break down during disaster or social collapse.

## 5.1.9.C — Principles and Ideals Shaping Government

Students compare survival decisions against common good, liberty, leadership, citizenship, and rule-based order.

## 5.2.9.A — Rights and Responsibilities in Systems of Government

Students consider how emergency conditions change the balance between individual rights, group safety, authority, and responsibility.

## 5.2.9.B — Citizens’ Rights and Responsibilities

Students evaluate what people owe each other during crisis: protection, cooperation, honesty, labor, obedience, dissent, and care.

## 5.2.9.C — Conflict Resolution

Team survival scenarios require students to resolve disagreements over scarce resources, leadership, safety, risk, and group priorities.

## 5.2.9.D — Political Leadership and Public Service

Students classify leadership styles, evaluate leadership effectiveness, and consider public responsibility under emergency conditions.

## 5.2.9.G — Civic Participation

Students design community responses, evaluate group roles, and consider how ordinary people participate in maintaining order and survival.

## 5.3.9.A — Structure and Operation of Government

Students connect disaster response, public safety, quarantine, emergency services, and government authority to realistic crisis planning.

## 5.3.9.K — Systems of Government

Student survival societies can be compared as systems of rule, authority, decision-making, and accountability.

## 5.4.9.A / 5.4.9.B — Nation-States and World Affairs

Students analyze border control, international aid, global health response, refugees, containment, and international cooperation.

# History

## 8.1.9.A — Chronological Thinking

Students construct timelines of outbreaks, responses, social collapse, recovery, and historical comparisons.

## 8.1.9.B — Historical Sources

Students use research libraries, historical examples, disaster studies, psychology sources, and media examples to support arguments.

## 8.1.9.C — Historical Interpretation

Students compare zombie origins, genre interpretations, public fears, and competing explanations of social behavior.

## 8.1.9.D — Historical Research

Students build supported claims from readings, examples, and evidence rather than unsupported opinion.

## 8.4.9.A — World History: Individuals and Groups

Students explore folklore, religion, colonial contexts, cultural transmission, and how societies explain death, fear, disease, and social control.

## 8.4.9.B — World History: Documents, Artifacts, and Historic Sites

Students examine folklore, oral traditions, cultural artifacts, and written sources related to zombies, monsters, disease, and fear.

## 8.4.9.C / 8.4.9.D — Continuity, Change, Conflict, and Cooperation

Students examine how disasters, invasions, epidemics, migrations, quarantines, violence, and group cooperation alter societies.

# Geography

## 7.1.9.A — Geographic Tools and Spatial Perspective

Students map outbreak movement, safe zones, supply routes, shelter locations, migration, barriers, and risk areas.

## 7.2 / 7.3 / 7.4 — Places, Regions, Human Characteristics, and Human-Environment Interaction

Students evaluate how terrain, population density, transportation, resources, infrastructure, land use, and settlement patterns affect survival strategy and social organization.

# Reading and Writing in History/Social Studies

## CC.8.5.6-8.A / CC.8.5.9-10.A — Cite Textual Evidence

Students cite specific textual evidence to support analysis of primary and secondary sources.

## CC.8.5.6-8.B / CC.8.5.9-10.B — Central Ideas and Summary

Students determine central ideas and summarize sources accurately.

## CC.8.5.6-8.F / CC.8.5.9-10.F — Author’s Point of View and Purpose

Students distinguish between scientific, historical, cultural, and entertainment-based explanations of zombies and fear.

## CC.8.5.6-8.G / CC.8.5.9-10.G — Integrating Diverse Sources

Students combine maps, videos, articles, rubrics, research libraries, and scenario data.

## CC.8.5.6-8.H / CC.8.5.9-10.H — Fact, Opinion, Reasoning, and Evidence

Students separate unsupported opinion from defensible, evidence-backed argument.

## CC.8.5.6-8.I / CC.8.5.9-10.I — Comparing Sources

Students compare folklore, psychology, disaster research, government planning, and pop-culture zombie types.

# Psychology and Social Science Connections

Pennsylvania Social Studies includes psychology as a social-studies course area. Zombie Invasion Prep uses psychology and social science to examine how individuals and groups respond to fear, danger, uncertainty, and social breakdown.

## Human Behavior Under Stress

Fear, anxiety, threat detection, survival behavior, group response, panic, resilience.

## Memory and Cognition

Episodic memory, procedural memory, emotional memory, recognition, decision-making, impulse control.

## Leadership and Group Behavior

Leadership styles, cooperation, conflict, social roles, authority, conformity, group survival.

## Social Norms and Taboo

Cannibalism, violence, group membership, moral restraint, trust, and social collapse.

## Adolescent Development

Teen decision-making, peer influence, risk perception, stress response, and long-term effects of crisis.
